**Why this wins right now.** Budgets for AI are enormous and still growing, but results are rare. MIT Project NANDA found roughly 95 percent of enterprise GenAI pilots produce no measurable P\&L impact, and RAND puts overall AI project failure above 80 percent. Forrester and Anaconda's 2026 research found 88 percent of agent pilots never reach production. Yet per BCG's 2026 AI Radar, 94 percent of executives will keep investing anyway. That combination (mandate to act, money allocated, repeated failure, no idea why) is the single best market condition a consultant can walk into. The buried finding in the MIT research is the whole pitch: externally led deployments succeeded about twice as often as internal builds. Companies literally get better AI results when someone like Sheila runs the implementation.

**Why Sheila specifically.** Most "AI consultants" have never owned a revenue number. Most revenue leaders have never shipped AI. Sheila has done both: accountable for a $30M+ ARR portfolio at New Relic, eliminated $21M in churn risk, delivered $3.1M in net-new committed growth, hit a 100 percent renewal rate across 200+ partners at Datto, built founding CS functions from zero, and separately solo-built and ships a live multi-tenant AI platform (simplilux.com, including the Atlas planning agent) that runs 28+ active projects with a 42 percent cycle-time reduction. That intersection (operator plus builder) is the moat. It cannot be faked and it is what buyers say they cannot find.

## 2\. Market context: why now

Five conditions make 2026 unusually favorable for exactly this practice.

**2.1 The AI results gap is the story of the year.** Adoption is near universal (McKinsey has \~88 percent of organizations using AI in at least one function) but value capture is rare (only \~39 percent report any EBIT impact; roughly 6 percent qualify as high performers). MIT's 95 percent stat, however people argue about its methodology, did one thing decisively: it made boards anxious and gave every CEO permission to admit their AI efforts aren't working. Anxious buyers with allocated budget and a public excuse to ask for help are the ideal client.

**2.2 The failure causes are exactly Sheila's skill set.** Across MIT, RAND, Forrester, and Deloitte the failure drivers repeat: unclear success criteria, no workflow integration, weak adoption and change management, nobody owning the outcome. Deloitte attributes roughly 70 percent of failures to people and process, only about 10 percent to the algorithms. These are not ML problems. They are operating problems, which is what a CS and RevOps operator solves for a living. The market's biggest complaint is a job description for Sheila.

**2.3 Retention is the board's new favorite metric.** Bain's Tech Report found NRR declined at 75 percent of software companies even as \~60 percent of them increased CS spending, because spend went to detection tooling instead of customer outcomes. TSIA's State of Customer Success 2026 frames the era plainly: CS must prove financial impact, and retention is the most reliable path to profitability. Meanwhile Forrester reports companies that embed AI properly into CS workflows see meaningful churn reduction in year one. Translation: CS leaders are under pressure, under-resourced, and being told to fix it with AI they don't know how to deploy. That is offers 3 and 4\.

**2.4 Fractional leadership went mainstream.** Roughly a third of US companies used a fractional leader in 2025, with projections above 40 percent of small and mid-market companies by end of 2026, and typical retainers of $3K-$10K per month. Buyers no longer need the model explained. Fractional CS specifically is underserved relative to fractional CFO and CMO, which are crowded. First-mover room exists.

**2.5 Pipeline generation is being rebuilt around AI.** SDR-style agent deployments show the fastest payback of any agent category (BCG and Forrester put median payback around 3.4 months). "GTM engineering" (signal-based, AI-personalized, systematized outbound) is the fastest-growing revenue discipline, but most SMBs cannot hire for it. A productized build-and-transfer offer meets that demand without Sheila becoming an agency.

**The honest risks in this market** (mitigations in Section 11): AI-consulting is getting crowded with thin operators, buyer skepticism is rising because of them, and platform vendors keep shipping features that absorb point solutions. The answer to all three is the same: sell measurable business outcomes tied to revenue, publish real numbers, and stay tool-agnostic.

## 3\. Positioning

**One-line positioning.** *"I help B2B teams make AI actually pay: keeping the customers you have and filling the pipeline you don't."*

**The spearhead rule.** The practice has three service lines but ONE marketing message. Everything public leads with the AI-results-gap story (the 95 percent problem) and lands on revenue outcomes. CS and Pipeline are presented as the two highest-ROI places to apply AI, not as separate businesses. This keeps the brand coherent while the offers stay broad enough to catch demand from any of the three directions the user cares about.

**Who it's for.**

- Primary: B2B SaaS and tech-enabled services companies, 10-200 employees, $1M-$50M revenue, North America. Founder-led or with a small exec team. They have revenue, customers, and pressure, but no CS ops muscle, no AI owner, and no time.  
- Secondary: established non-tech SMBs ($2M-$50M, professional services, distribution, multi-location services) whose leadership keeps saying "we should be doing something with AI" and privately has no idea what. They enter through the Audit.

**Who it's not for** (say this publicly; qualification is a trust signal and takeaway selling works): pre-revenue startups, companies wanting a cheap pair of hands to "do AI stuff," anyone who wants a strategy deck instead of working systems, and regulated use cases needing formal ML engineering (refer out).

**Against the alternatives.**

| Alternative | Their weakness | Sheila's counter |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Big consultancies | 5-10x price, junior delivery, decks not systems | Senior operator does the work, fixed fees, ships in weeks |
| Freelance AI devs | Build tools nobody adopts, no revenue context | Adoption and change management are the core deliverable |
| "AI agencies" | Hype, templated chatbots, churn | Tool-agnostic, outcome-priced, guarantee on the Audit |
| DIY internal | \~2x failure rate vs externally led (MIT) | Cite that stat, offer the Audit as the cheap way to find out |
| Do nothing | Competitors compound learning | Loss framing: every quarter of waiting is margin someone else banks |

**Proof assets, in order of deployment.** (1) Sheila's operator numbers, on the site from day one. (2) The live simplilux.com build as shipped-AI evidence. (3) The meta-proof: her own client acquisition runs on the Pipeline Engine she sells. (4) Founding-client case studies with named results by day 90, which become the primary asset for months 4-12.

**Niche checkpoint.** Do not pre-narrow beyond the ICP above. At day 60, review every conversation and closed deal, and narrow the public message toward wherever two or more clients cluster (likely either "AI for CS teams at B2B SaaS" or "AI operations for services SMBs"). Data decides, not guessing.

## 4\. Service architecture and delivery specs

The ladder is designed so every engagement creates the need for the next one, and the first step is small enough to say yes to in one call.

### 4.1 AI Clarity Audit ($4,500 fixed, 2 weeks) \- the wedge

**Promise:** "Know exactly where AI pays off in your business, in 14 days, with one automation already running before the report lands." **Week 1:** Kickoff (90 min), 4-6 stakeholder interviews, workflow and tooling inventory, data access review, time-and-cost mapping of the 10 most repeated workflows. **Week 2:** Build one quick-win automation live in their stack (this is the differentiator; nobody else ships during a diagnostic). Score every opportunity on ROI, effort, risk, and adoption likelihood. Deliver: Opportunity Map (ranked), 90-Day Roadmap, build/buy/skip calls per item, and a 60-minute leadership readout. **Guarantee:** If the audit doesn't surface at least three opportunities each worth 10x the fee within 12 months, full refund. (Risk reversal that costs nothing in practice because the bar is genuinely easy to clear, and it collapses the buying decision.) **Founding-client program:** first three audits at $3,500 in exchange for a written case study, a testimonial, and two introductions. Frame it as selective, not desperate: "I'm choosing three founding clients this quarter." **Conversion target:** 50-60 percent of audits convert to a Sprint, Build, or retainer, because the roadmap is literally a proposal.

### 4.2 AI Implementation Sprint ($9,500 per 30-day sprint)

Priced deliberately under $10K because that is a common sign-off threshold; it lets a VP buy without a procurement cycle. Scope: 2-3 workflows from the roadmap shipped into production (examples: inbound lead triage and routing, meeting-notes-to-CRM pipeline, customer health digest, onboarding automation, support deflection with human handoff, reporting automation). Includes SOPs, recorded team training, an adoption scorecard, and 2 weeks of post-sprint support. Success criteria defined in business metrics before work starts (per the research: projects with pre-agreed metrics succeed at dramatically higher rates). Bundle: 3 sprints for $25,500.

### 4.3 Customer Success System Build ($12,000, 6 weeks)

For companies with real customers and no real CS system. Deliverables: customer journey and onboarding redesign (time-to-first-value as the north star), health scoring model wired to their actual data, renewal and expansion playbooks, QBR/EBR cadence and templates, escalation paths, CS dashboard, plus an AI-assist layer (risk digests, call summaries to CRM, renewal-prep briefs). Positioned with the Bain finding: most CS spend fails because it buys detection, not outcomes; this build is outcome-first.

### 4.4 Fractional CS Leadership ($5,000 / month, 3-month minimum)

Roughly a day-a-week equivalent, scoped by outcomes not hours: own the retention number, run the health and risk cadence, coach whoever touches customers, report to the exec team monthly. Market-normal pricing for fractional leadership ($3K-$10K band) at the credible middle. Cap at 2 concurrent fractional clients to protect delivery.

### 4.5 Pipeline Engine ($8,500 build over 3-4 weeks, optional $2,500 / month managed)

The productized version of Sheila's own acquisition system: ICP definition, signal strategy (hiring, funding, leadership changes, tech installs), data sourcing (Lusha or client's tools), messaging system in the client's voice, AI research and personalization workflow, sequencing with deliverability guardrails and CASL/CAN-SPAM compliance, CRM hygiene, and a reporting loop. Ends with a transfer week training their team, or rolls into managed mode. Sales line that closes it: "You're reading proof it works."

### 4.6 Advisory Retainer ($2,000 / month)

Two calls monthly plus async access. Offered at the end of every project as the default "what happens next." Low effort, high margin, keeps the relationship warm for the next project.

**Delivery guardrails (all offers):** written success criteria before kickoff; weekly written update every Friday; everything documented so the client owns it after; never fabricate a capability, timeline, or metric; tool-agnostic recommendations with reasoning shown.

## 5\. Pricing strategy and commercial terms

**Principles applied.**

- Fixed fees, not hourly. SMB buyers hate meter anxiety; certainty closes. Hourly also punishes Sheila for being fast with AI leverage.  
- Anchor value against the cost of the problem, in every proposal. Example math to reuse: a $3M ARR company at 85 percent gross retention loses $450K a year; moving retention 5 points is worth $150K annually, against a $12K build. A single mid-market client saved pays for years of fees. Make the client do this math out loud on the call.  
- Price ladder psychology: small first yes ($4.5K) before the big ask (commitment and consistency), sub-$10K sprint (sign-off threshold), monthly retainers (predictability for both sides).  
- Scarcity that's true: solo capacity is 3-4 concurrent clients. Publish it ("I take four clients at a time"). Never invent fake urgency; real constraint is more persuasive anyway.  
- Risk reversal only on the Audit. Implementation work doesn't get a refund guarantee; it gets defined success criteria and weekly transparency instead.

**Terms.** 50 percent to book, 50 percent on delivery for projects; retainers billed monthly in advance; all via Stripe payment links (card or ACH/PAD). Net 0 on the deposit: the calendar slot is reserved when payment lands. Price increases: raise all rates \~20 percent after every three closed clients or whenever booked beyond 70 percent capacity, whichever comes first. Founding pricing dies permanently at client \#4; announce that in advance (deadline effect, honestly earned).

**Currency.** Quote USD by default (most buyers will be US; also aligns with US-referenced rates for senior talent). Offer CAD at spot for Canadian clients. Confirm cross-border tax handling with an accountant (exported services to US clients are generally zero-rated for GST/HST, but get it confirmed).

## 6\. Ideal client profile, personas, and buying triggers

**Firmographics (primary).** B2B SaaS and tech-enabled services; 10-200 employees; $1M-$50M revenue; US and Canada; founder-led or lean exec team; has paying customers and at least one of: churn anxiety, a stalled AI initiative, or a pipeline problem.

**Personas.**

1. **Founder/CEO (10-60 employees).** Feels board or peer pressure about AI, embarrassed by lack of progress, protective of cash. Buys the Audit. Cares about: not looking foolish, fast visible wins, not adding headcount. Message that lands: "clarity in two weeks, one automation live before the report, guaranteed."  
2. **VP CS / Head of Customer Experience (40-200 employees).** Told to "do more with less, use AI." Owns NRR. Buys the CS Build or Fractional. Cares about: defending the function, board-ready retention story, team not burning out. Message: "turn your CS data into a retention number the board applauds."  
3. **CEO/CRO without an SDR function.** Referral-dependent pipeline, allergic to agency retainers after being burned. Buys the Pipeline Engine. Message: "own the engine instead of renting an agency; this email is the demo."

**Buying trigger signals to hunt (these drive the outbound engine, Section 7.3).**

- Hiring a CSM, CS Manager, or first CS hire (churn pain plus budget, and a 60-90 day window before the hire ramps)  
- Hiring anything with "AI" in the title, or an exec posting publicly about AI plans (mandate without know-how)  
- New funding round in the last 90 days (budget, board pressure)  
- New VP Sales / VP CS in the last 90 days (new leaders buy new systems)  
- Layoffs in CS/sales (survivors need leverage, "do more with less" is literal)  
- Posting SDR roles repeatedly (pipeline pain, open to a system instead of a hire)

## 7\. Go-to-market plan: four organic engines

No ads, ever, per constraint. Every engine below is buildable this month and largely automatable with the existing stack (Claude cowork \+ Lusha \+ Gmail/Outlook \+ Resend \+ LinkedIn done manually).

### 7.1 Engine 1: Warm network activation (fastest path to clients 1-3)

The first clients will not come from strangers. Week 1-2: build a list of 75-100 past colleagues, clients, partners, and vendor contacts. Send personal, individually written notes (templates in the Execution Checklist) announcing the practice with ONE specific ask: "who do you know who keeps saying 'we should do something with AI' but hasn't?" Specific asks get answers; "keep me in mind" gets silence. Include the founding-client framing (three slots, reduced rate, case study exchange). Psychology at work: reciprocity (open with genuine personal interest), social proof (real numbers in the announcement), scarcity (three slots), and the Ben Franklin effect (asking a small favor deepens the relationship). Target: 100 touches, 30 conversations, 10 referrals, 2-3 founding clients within 45 days.

### 7.2 Engine 2: LinkedIn founder-brand content engine

LinkedIn is where every persona above lives, and organic reach still rewards genuine operator content. Cadence: 3 posts per week (sustainable beats ambitious), 10 thoughtful comments per day on posts by ICP buyers and large adjacent accounts (comments are the underrated distribution channel; they put Sheila's name in front of the exact audience free), and 5 value-first DMs per day to engaged commenters. Content pillars: (a) build teardowns with receipts ("I automated X in 45 minutes, here is the exact workflow"), (b) the AI reality-gap narrative with the 95 percent stat and what the 5 percent do differently, (c) CS and retention math posts, (d) honest build-in-public notes from the practice, (e) one soft offer post per month. Batch production: Monday morning, one hour with Claude drafting from Sheila's voice notes; Sheila edits so every post stays authentically hers. First 12 post briefs are pre-written in the Execution Checklist so month one requires zero ideation.

### 7.3 Engine 3: Signal-based outbound (dogfooding the Pipeline Engine)

Volume: 15-25 new contacts per day, 4-5 days a week, every one tied to a specific trigger from Section 6\. House rules carried over from the existing Simplilux playbook because they work: short emails (half normal length), open on the recipient's KPI not on the sender, Tuesday-Thursday sends 8-11am recipient-local, staggered 20+ minutes apart, widening-gap follow-up sequence, hard stop on any human reply, CASL-compliant identification and unsubscribe, and never fabricate a claim. Sequences and stop rules are fully written in the Execution Checklist. Weekly expectation once warm: 80-100 new contacts, 8-12 replies, 3-5 conversations, 1-2 discovery calls. Deliverability setup (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, warm ramp) is a day-one task; a burned domain is a burned month.

### 7.4 Engine 4: Referral partnerships and communities

Partners who see the pain first and don't sell what Sheila sells: fractional CFOs and CMOs, bookkeepers and accounting firms, MSPs and IT providers, dev shops, SaaS implementation agencies, and business coaches. Offer a clean 10 percent referral fee or reciprocal referrals, plus a co-branded "AI Reality Check" workshop they can gift their clients (reciprocity at the partner level). Communities: Gain Grow Retain and CS-focused Slack groups, RevGenius, Pavilion (paid, evaluate at month 3), TechTO and GTA founder circles for local density, plus relevant subreddits and forums answered helpfully, never pitchy. One free 45-minute webinar per month ("Why your AI pilot will fail, and the 3 that won't") doubles as content, lead capture, and partner co-marketing.

### 7.5 Supporting assets

- **Lead magnet:** "The AI Reality Check," a 20-question scorecard that predicts whether an AI initiative will pay off. Gated by email (double opt-in via Resend, CASL-clean). It qualifies leads while teaching the sales narrative.  
- **Newsletter:** biweekly, one teardown or one retention play per issue, 300-500 words. List builds slowly; its job is nurture, not reach.  
- **Website \+ SEO:** covered in the Website Plan. Long-tail intent terms only ("AI readiness assessment for small business," "fractional customer success," "AI audit consultant"); treat SEO as a compounding side effect of repurposed content, not a channel to force.

## 8\. Delivery system and methodology

**The house framework, used everywhere: Diagnose, Build, Adopt.** Diagnose: map the workflow, quantify the cost of the current state, define success in business metrics before any build. Build: smallest system that moves the metric, in the client's stack, documented as it's built. Adopt: training, SOPs, an owner named on the client side, a 30-day adoption scorecard. Adoption is the differentiator because adoption is where the market fails (see Section 2.2); say so constantly.

**Reusable delivery assets to create once (Execution Checklist schedules these):** audit interview guide, workflow-inventory template, opportunity scoring matrix, roadmap deck skeleton, sprint plan template, SOP template, training session outline, health-score model starter, renewal playbook skeleton, outbound engine runbook, weekly client update template. Every asset is a ClickUp template so each new client is a duplicated workspace, not a blank page.

**Quality bar.** Friday written updates without being asked. Show the work (loom walkthroughs of builds). Flag risks early and in writing. If something fails, say so plainly and fix it; trust compounds faster than perfection.

## 12\. 12-month roadmap, KPIs, and decision checkpoints

**Days 1-14: Foundation.** Registration, banking, Stripe, domain, site live via Codex, sales assets, deliverability setup, warm list built and first 40 notes sent, first 6 posts published. **Days 15-45: First revenue.** Founding audits sold and delivered brilliantly, outbound engine live at 15/day, webinar \#1, first partner conversations, case study \#1 drafted the day audit \#1 ends. **Days 46-90: Proof and ladder.** Convert audits to sprints/builds, publish case study \#1, raise to full pricing at client \#4, niche decision at day 60, first retainer signed. **Months 4-6: Compounding.** 2 retainers live, referral loop formalized (ask every happy client for two intros at the final readout; the moment of peak reciprocity), monthly webinar cadence, newsletter at 300+ subscribers. **Months 7-12: Optimize.** Price raise \#2, kill the weakest offer, double down where revenue clusters, evaluate Pavilion/paid communities, begin one leveraged asset (self-serve audit lite or paid workshop) only if delivery is under control.

**Weekly KPI scoreboard (log every Friday, 10 minutes):** posts published (3), comments (50), new outbound contacts (80-100), replies, conversations started (5+), discovery calls held (2+), proposals sent, revenue closed, pipeline value, and one sentence on what worked.

**Decision checkpoints.**

- **Day 30:** at least 1 founding client signed or 5+ discovery calls held. If neither, the problem is the warm-network execution, not the market; rerun Engine 1 before touching the offer.  
- **Day 60:** niche review. Narrow public messaging toward the cluster in the data.  
- **Day 90:** founding pricing ends, case study \#1 live, raise rates, decide whether Pipeline Engine or CS Build becomes the flagship based on close rates and delivery joy.
